# TailorGS: Made-to-Measure Density Control for 3D Gaussian Splatting

- Date: 2026-09-25
- Authors: Afif Boudaoud, Jiayi Liu, Alexandru Calotoiu, Torsten Hoefler
- Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31248v2
- Tag: segmentation

3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructs a scene from a set of posed photographs called the capture; Gaussian count affects reconstruction quality, storage, and rendering cost. How to select this number automatically across capture scales remains unresolved: density control techniques effective on standard benchmarks can leave larger captures with too few Gaussians to reconstruct fine details. We observe that the surface to represent, given by capture extent and resolution, is known before training, whereas the capacity needed to reconstruct its content depends strongly on the 3D optimization trajectory and becomes apparent during reconstruction. We introduce TailorGS, which unifies coverage-scaled exploration and coverage-weighted self-distillation under a non-redundant 3D scene coverage measure. Before training, TailorGS groups observed surface points into coarse voxels and shares each voxel's weight among views observing it from the same side. Combined with image resolution, these weights set a generous exploration cap that limits budget inflation from repeated observations. During the first 80% of training, TailorGS grows toward this cap. The resulting model serves as teacher for a single closed-form self-distillation step, balancing fidelity to its renderings against Gaussian count under the same coverage measure. The retained primitives are refined over the remaining 20% of training. On 13 standard scenes, TailorGS matches the mean PSNR of the best-performing evaluated baseline, LeGS, with 48% fewer Gaussians. On eight large captures, the same configuration automatically scales to larger models when necessary, achieving the highest mean PSNR across 13 evaluated baselines: 0.63 dB above the runner-up by allocating 2.8 times as many Gaussians.
